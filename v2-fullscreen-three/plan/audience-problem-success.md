# v2 Audience, Problem, and Success Model

Scott clarified two key planning points:

1. The audience is people who may want to hire him.
2. The site must clarify the problem it fixes, how success is measured, and why the problem matters.

This document anchors v2 around buyer intent instead of visual novelty alone.

---

## Primary audience

The primary audience is **people evaluating whether to hire Scott** for high-leverage technical work.

### Audience segments

1. **Founders / startup leaders**
   - Need a senior technical partner to turn AI ideas into real product systems.
   - Care about speed, quality, technical judgment, and avoiding expensive wrong turns.

2. **Engineering leaders / CTOs**
   - Need help modernizing complex systems without pausing delivery.
   - Care about architecture, team leverage, security, reliability, and maintainability.

3. **Product leaders / operators**
   - Need AI-enabled workflows connected to business outcomes.
   - Care about user value, delivery risk, roadmap clarity, and measurable impact.

4. **Strategic partners / collaborators**
   - Need a systems-minded builder for agentic AI, local-first workflows, and product architecture.
   - Care about taste, credibility, range, and whether Scott thinks differently.

### What they need to believe

By the end of the site, a qualified visitor should believe:

- Scott understands complex technical systems.
- Scott can translate AI possibility into business outcomes.
- Scott can modernize without creating chaos.
- Scott has enough taste and originality to be valuable in ambiguous work.
- Scott is not just a developer — he is a product-driven systems architect and agentic engineering partner.

---

## Problem the site is fixing

The site itself fixes a positioning and trust problem:

> People who need AI systems, modernization, or technical leadership often cannot tell from a normal portfolio whether someone can handle ambiguous, high-stakes systems work.

A traditional portfolio says:

- here are projects
- here are tools
- here are services
- contact me

But Scott's target buyer needs more than that. They need evidence of:

- systems thinking
- judgment
- ability to reduce complexity
- technical depth
- business context
- unique perspective
- ability to ship

### Buyer-side problem

The visitor is likely asking:

- Can this person help us make sense of AI without wasting months?
- Can this person modernize a messy platform without breaking the business?
- Can this person lead architecture while still understanding product?
- Can this person work across agents, local LLMs, edge workflows, web/mobile, and legacy systems?
- Is this person credible enough for a consulting engagement, fractional architect role, or strategic partnership?

### Scott-side problem

Scott's old positioning can be misread as:

- mobile developer
- general software engineer
- web/app builder
- portfolio of past projects only

v2 should reposition him as:

- Agentic Engineering lead
- AI systems architect
- local-first AI strategist
- modernization partner
- product-driven technical leader

---

## Core promise

A possible core promise for v2:

> I help teams turn AI ambition and legacy complexity into secure, local-first, production-ready systems.

Alternative sharper versions:

1. **For founders:**
   > Turn AI ideas into production systems without wasting months on fragile prototypes.

2. **For CTOs:**
   > Modernize complex platforms and introduce agentic AI without disrupting delivery.

3. **For product leaders:**
   > Connect autonomous AI workflows to real business outcomes, not demos.

4. **For strategic partners:**
   > Build local-first, agentic systems that are private, resilient, and useful.

---

## Why this is important

The site should make the stakes obvious:

- AI prototypes are easy; production AI systems are hard.
- Teams can burn months on demos that never become durable workflows.
- Legacy systems slow delivery but cannot always be replaced all at once.
- Privacy, data control, and local-first architecture are becoming strategic advantages.
- Autonomous agents need orchestration, evaluation, guardrails, and product judgment.
- The wrong architecture creates long-term drag; the right architecture creates leverage.

The visitor should feel:

> This matters because our technical decisions now will either compound into leverage or become expensive entropy.

## How to show the user this is important

### 1. Visual metaphor

Use the fullscreen Three.js system to dramatize the problem:

- scattered blocks = disconnected tools / legacy complexity
- noisy swarm = ungoverned AI experimentation
- flowing water = business process and operational fit
- flower-of-life = autonomy and coherent patterns
- tree/root system = durable architecture and growth
- constellations = shipped proof and connected outcomes

The animation should not be random decoration. It should show transformation:

```text
chaos → orchestration → useful system → business outcome
```

### 2. Narrative structure

Each screen should answer one buyer question:

1. **Why should I care?**
   AI and legacy complexity are creating real delivery risk and opportunity.

2. **What problem do you solve?**
   Scott turns complexity into secure, local-first, agentic systems that ship.

3. **Why are you different?**
   Systems thinking + product leadership + hands-on implementation + agentic AI depth.

4. **Can you prove it?**
   Shipped products, modernization patterns, AI workflows, field notes, and concrete examples.

5. **What should I do next?**
   Book a conversation / discuss a system / start with an architecture review.

### 3. Content mechanics

Use concise but concrete copy:

- problem-first headlines
- one-line stakes
- proof points
- decision-maker language
- keyword-rich field notes
- clear offer paths

Avoid leading with tools. Tools support the story, but the buyer cares about outcomes.

## Success metrics

### Business success

Measure whether the site creates better-fit opportunities:

- qualified inquiry count
- consulting/fractional architect leads
- strategic partnership conversations
- Calendly clicks
- LinkedIn contact clicks
- inbound messages referencing AI systems / modernization / local-first AI

### Positioning success

Measure whether visitors understand the intended positioning:

- Can a visitor describe Scott as more than a mobile/web developer?
- Do they use language like `AI systems`, `agentic engineering`, `architecture`, `modernization`, or `local-first` when they reach out?
- Do they understand what problem Scott solves within the first screen?

### Product/site success

Measure whether the experience works as a site:

- mobile engagement depth
- time on page
- scroll/state completion
- CTA click-through
- no horizontal overflow
- fast load on mobile
- accessible semantic content without relying entirely on WebGL
- reduced-motion support

### Creative success

Measure whether uniqueness helps, not hurts:

- visitors remember the site
- visual system reinforces the message
- Three.js scenes clarify transformation instead of distracting
- recruiters/general visitors may enjoy it, but hiring decision-makers still understand the offer

---

## Recommended north-star metric

The strongest north-star metric is not raw traffic.

It is:

> Qualified conversations with people who understand Scott as an Agentic Engineering / AI Systems Architecture partner.

A good v2 should generate fewer generic leads and more high-fit conversations.

---

## Planning implications

1. Start every major section with a buyer problem, not a personal biography.
2. Make the 3D world explain transformation, not just show beauty.
3. Keep semantic text strong for accessibility and search.
4. Make CTAs specific: `Discuss Your AI System`, `Book an Architecture Review`, `Explore Modernization Paths`.
5. Use proof to reduce perceived hiring risk.
6. Preserve uniqueness, but do not let novelty obscure the offer.
